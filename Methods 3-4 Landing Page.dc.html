Meet Aisha Patel

Head of VCE Maths Methods

"Methods rewards systems, not heroics. We teach every topic before your school does, then drill it until exam pressure feels familiar. Come sit in a class. You'll feel the difference in twenty minutes."

I sat Methods as a Year 11, a year early, and I remember exactly how it felt: I was a good maths student who suddenly had no idea whether I was actually prepared. My teacher was lovely but stretched across thirty of us. The textbook questions looked nothing like my SACs. And nobody could answer the one question I cared about: am I on track?

That question is why I teach this subject the way I do. Methods is the most systematic subject in VCE: a fixed study design, a predictable exam, question types that repeat year after year. Which means preparation can be engineered. Learn each topic before school touches it, so class becomes your second pass. Drill the algebra until the tech-free paper feels slow. Build your CAS workflows early, so Exam 2 becomes a time surplus instead of a time crisis. Rehearse every SAC before you sit it. None of that is heroic. It is a system, executed weekly.

That is what my team builds around you here. Every tutor on this page scored 45+ raw in Methods, most within the last few years, so when they show you how to set out a probability question, it is because that exact setting-out earned them marks. And when you message the text line at 9pm, someone who has sat this exam answers you.

My promise is simple. Do the week, week after week, and you will walk into November knowing exactly what to expect, because you will have already done it dozens of times. No surprises. No heroics. A score that reflects the work. I hope to meet you in a trial class soon.

Section A · Introduction to Transformations

Image and pre-image

Every transformation question starts with two coordinates: the point you begin with, and the point it lands on. The original coordinate is the pre-image; the transformed coordinate is the image.

Transformations set the foundation for Family of Functions, a very common SAC and exam question. Lock this in early.

Notation

Pre-image: (x, y)  →  Image: (x′, y′)

Learning objectives

[1.3.1]  Apply x′ and y′ notation to transform points, interpret transformations, and handle altered orders.

[1.3.2]  Find transformed functions.

[1.3.3]  Find the transformations from the transformed function: reverse engineering.

NOTE: The x′ and y′ notation will be used quite heavily!

Weekly Workbook · page 4

Exploration · Dilation

Plot the transformed points!

P₁  Dilation by factor 2 from the x-axis  (x, y) → (x, 2y)

P₂  Dilation by factor ½ from the x-axis  (x, y) → (x, ½y)

P₃  Dilation by factor 2 from the y-axis  (x, y) → (2x, y)

P₄  Dilation by factor ½ from the y-axis  (x, y) → (½x, y)

Question 2 · Your turn

Find the image (x′, y′) after applying to (x, y): a dilation by factor 3 from the x-axis, then a dilation by factor ⅕ from the y-axis.

NOTE: Always say dilation FROM an axis, and reflection IN an axis.

Exam-style set

Tech-free 12 minutes

1. It is known that (2, 3) transformed into (4, 5). State the value of x′ and y′. (1 mark)

2. Find the transformations that map y = x² onto y = −3(x − 2)² + 1. (4 marks)
